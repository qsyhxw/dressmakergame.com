Dressmaker Customer Guide – Style Preferences & Requirements

Learn the confirmed full-game customer-order loop, which characters have official major-commission achievements, and which detailed style-tag tips apply only to Prototype 0.5.5.

⚠️ Version Boundary

The full-game overview and character names are taken from official Steam materials. The three launch-day commission examples below come from a dated hands-on walkthrough and are not a universal scoring formula. The detailed scoring, tag, budget, and troubleshooting sections are preserved as Prototype 0.5.5 observations. Do not carry prototype rules or workarounds into the complete Steam release without rechecking them in your build; no official complete scoring formula is published.

Prototype fact check: Repeating a customer conversation does not reset customer patience in Prototype 0.5.5; do not rely on that workaround.

🎯 Quick Reference: Satisfying Customers

  1. 1. Full game: Read each townsperson's tastes and event context, design the dress, choose fabric, cut, sew, decorate, and turn it in. For the first three documented examples, use the launch-day combinations below as a starting point.
  2. 2. Prototype tag rule: In v0.5.5, fabric tags drive the style score; patterns and accessories were not counted the same way.
  3. 3. Prototype quality rule: Budget, sewing accuracy, and grain alignment affect the legacy commission result.
  4. 4. Do not invent a universal formula: Verify the released build's visible requirements instead of assuming prototype weights.

Full-Game Customer Orders: What Is Confirmed

The official Steam description says the complete game is built around townsfolk with individual tastes and important events. The supported loop is: read the commission, choose dress pieces and fabrics, arrange and cut the pattern, sew the garment, add decorations, and turn in the finished dress for reputation and coin. The game also lets you sell pre-made dresses through the shop window, which is separate from a named story commission.

Officially Named Major-Commission Characters

Light name spoilers: Steam's public achievement descriptions confirm major-commission lines for these characters:

Alice, Rose, Dorothy, Marie, Fiona, Vivian, Edith, Adora, Prudence, and Priya.

This is a verified achievement-linked roster, not a claim that these are the only customers in the game. Hidden achievement conditions and each character's exact preference formula are intentionally not guessed.

For a current order, prioritize the requirements shown in the released game's own commission and sketchbook UI. If a prototype tip below conflicts with that UI, the released build is authoritative.

After Build 403: summon the next customer and check payment

When you finish with a customer in the Steam release, ring the bell to summon the next one; waiting at the counter alone is no longer the next step. The developer also says Build 403 fixes a case where a failed commission paid nothing even though the character accepted it. First let Steam install the update, then check whether the character accepts the submitted dress and whether coin is awarded. If that exact accepted-but-unpaid result persists, report the build and commission rather than assuming an undocumented scoring rule.

This is the developer's Build 403 patch report ↗, not a site-tested payout guarantee. It does not establish a fix for three-colour requirements or every submission problem.

Launch-Day Commission Examples

A hands-on walkthrough published by Into Indie Games on September 21, 2026 documents workable starting choices for the first three commissions. These are examples, not the only valid dresses: the released game allows different pattern, fabric, and accessory combinations. Keep the commission requirements visible and treat a green in-game requirement as more current than any fixed web recipe.

First commission

Librarian Workwear

Build toward Professional. The documented route uses a princess bodice with a collar, peasant sleeves, and a pencil, princess, or straight long skirt.

  • Start with the cool grey linen already available because it adds Professional.
  • Recheck the draft total before buying extra fabric or decoration.

Funeral commission

Flamboyant Funeral

Build toward Glamour and Elegant while respecting the instruction to avoid black.

  • A sweetheart bodice, flounce sleeve, and mermaid skirt supply a useful draft base.
  • Use velvet to raise the missing attributes; elegant white lace is an optional finishing choice.

Funeral commission

Fierce Funeral

This brief reverses the colour condition: use black only and build toward Gothic plus Professional.

  • The documented draft starts with an elevated sweetheart bodice, pointed collar, flounce sleeve, and professional skirt.
  • Black-and-white polka linen, black corduroy, and black wool pinstripes can supply the required attributes; black trim can finish the look.

Use the examples without locking yourself into one recipe

Draft for the required attributes first, then use the Emporium filters to close any gap with fabric. Stay within budget and slow down on curved sewing lines. If a requirement will not register even when the UI shows the right choices, stop spending materials and check the troubleshooting guide rather than repeatedly rebuilding the same dress.

Walkthrough reference: Into Indie Games, “Dressmaker Tips And Commission Walkthrough” (September 21, 2026) ↗. The exact combinations above are a single published playthrough and may change after patches.

👗 Understanding Customer Requests

In the Dressmaker prototype, customers visit your shop with specific requests. Each commission includes:

Request Components

📅

Occasion

The event or context (e.g., "work dress," "fundraiser," "ball")

✨

Style Requirements

Tags like "professional," "cute," "elegant" that must be met

💰

Budget

Maximum amount you can spend on fabric

⭐

Quality Expectation

Minimum quality score required (achieved through sewing + grain alignment)

What Currently Matters (v0.5.5)

  • Fabric choice: The ONLY thing that affects style tags
  • Sewing quality: Follow dotted lines, align grain properly
  • Budget management: Don't overspend on materials

What Currently Doesn't Matter

  • Pattern selection: Dress shape doesn't affect style score (only fabric does)
  • Accessories: Bows, buttons, lace don't contribute to style points
  • Color preferences: No specific color requirements exist (choose freely)

⚠️ Common Frustration

In Prototype 0.5.5, this guide treats fabric tags as the scoring signal and accessories as visual decoration. Do not assume the same scoring behavior in the complete release without testing it there.

How Tags Work

  1. 1. View Tags in Shop: When browsing fabrics, you'll see small labels like "Cute +" or "Elegant ++" on each material.
  2. 2. Multiple Tags per Fabric: Some fabrics have 2-3 tags (e.g., a fabric might be "Cute + Romantic").
  3. 3. Tag Strength: More "+" symbols = stronger contribution to that style (【Note: exact scoring formula unconfirmed】).
  4. 4. Mix Fabrics: Use different fabrics for bodice/skirt/sleeves to combine tags and hit multiple requirements.

Budget Management Tips

  1. 1. Calculate Before Buying: The prototype does not show a universal total fabric requirement upfront. Draft first, group pieces by fabric, and use the Fabric Budget Guide before shopping.
  2. 2. Mix Cheap + Tagged: Use expensive tagged fabric for the most visible piece (bodice or skirt), cheaper matching fabric for other parts.
  3. 3. Avoid Waste: Efficient pattern placement saves money. Align grain properly (left/right arrows) to avoid recuts.
  4. 4. No Undo: If you overspend, you'll need to restart the dress (or entire game in prototype). Plan carefully!

😤 Common Frustrations & Workarounds

Accessories Don't Affect Style Score

Problem: You add cute bows to hit "cute" requirement, but they don't contribute to style points.

Why: Current prototype only counts fabric tags. Accessories are purely decorative.

Workaround: Ignore accessories for scoring purposes. Use them only for visual appeal.

Version boundary: The official full-release materials do not publish an accessory scoring formula.

Pattern Choice Doesn't Matter

Problem: You pick a clearly "elegant" neckline or "cute" collar, but it doesn't affect elegant/cute scores.

Why: Pattern pieces lack style tags in v0.5.5.

Workaround: Choose patterns for visual appeal and personal preference. Focus fabric selection for style requirements.

Version boundary: Recheck pattern scoring in the full release; no official formula is published.

No Color Preference System

Problem: Rose "evidently prefers green" but the game doesn't penalize non-green choices.

Why: Color preferences are not mechanically enforced in Prototype 0.5.5.

Workaround: Use implied preferences (like Rose + green) for immersion, but don't worry about penalties.

Version boundary: Individual tastes are part of the full-game description, but no official color-bonus formula is published.

Hard to Remember Which Fabrics Have Which Tags

Problem: You go back and forth between shop and sketchbook trying to remember tags.

Workaround (In-Game): Some players use the colored pencils to mark fabrics in sketchbook. Drag fabric samples into sketch for reference.

Workaround (External): Keep notes or screenshot the shop to reference tags.

Full-game change: The September 21 launch walkthrough documents Emporium filters for fabrics and accessories. Use those filters in the released build; the note-taking workaround remains relevant only to the prototype archive.

No Specific Guidance on What Customers Want

Problem: Request says "simple but cute" – what does that mean in fabric terms?

Why: Prototype has minimal customer dialogue/personality.

Workaround: Focus on the style tags listed in requirements. "Cute" = find "Cute +" fabrics. "Simple" = visual interpretation (solid colors, clean lines).

Version boundary: Official materials mention individual tastes and important events, not a confirmed relationship-level system.
